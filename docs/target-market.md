## Overview

RTCM serves organizations that sit in the gap between small businesses
managing controls manually through spreadsheets and large public companies
with enterprise GRC platforms and dedicated internal audit functions. This
gap — the U.S. mid-market — represents the largest underserved segment in
the financial controls technology market.

Three primary customer segments define RTCM's initial go-to-market focus,
each with distinct pain points, buying criteria, and addressable market size.

---

## Segment 1 — Private Equity Portfolio Companies

### Profile
- Revenue: $10M to $500M per entity
- Structure: Multiple legal entities under a shared PE sponsor
- Finance team: Typically one Controller or Director of Accounting
  per entity, with limited corporate oversight between audits
- Audit status: Annual external audit, not subject to SOX 404(b)
  auditor attestation

### The Problem They Have
Private equity sponsors acquire companies quickly and integrate them into
portfolios that may span 5 to 25 entities. Each entity has its own ERP,
its own chart of accounts, and its own control environment — but the PE
sponsor's finance team has no real-time visibility into control health
across the portfolio. Between annual audits, control failures accumulate
undetected.

Common failure modes in PE-backed portfolios:
- Segregation of duties conflicts created during rapid onboarding of
  acquired entities, never remediated
- Intercompany transactions recorded inconsistently across entities,
  discovered only at period-end consolidation
- Journal entries posted without adequate approval workflow because
  the legacy ERP at an acquired company does not enforce it
- Reconciliations that fall behind during acquisition integration and
  are never caught up

These failures become material when the sponsor pursues a refinancing,
add-on acquisition, or exit — and the buyer's due diligence team
identifies control deficiencies that require expensive remediation or
result in a price reduction.

### Why RTCM Fits
RTCM's multi-entity architecture is designed specifically for this
structure. A single dashboard shows control health across every entity
in the portfolio simultaneously. When Entity A has a self-approval
failure and Entity B has an intercompany imbalance, both appear in the
same alert feed — routed to the appropriate Controller at the entity
level and visible to the CFO at the portfolio level.

### Addressable Market
- Approximately 8,000 PE-backed U.S. companies with $10M–$500M revenue
  (PitchBook, 2024)
- Average of 6–8 entities per portfolio company at Professional tier
  pricing of $349/entity/month
- Estimated annual contract value per customer: $25,000–$35,000

## Segment 2 — Mid-Market Companies Preparing for Audit or Transaction

### Profile
- Revenue: $25M to $500M
- Structure: Single entity or small multi-entity (2–5 entities)
- Finance team: Controller or VP Finance, often without dedicated
  internal audit function
- Trigger event: Approaching first external audit, strategic sale,
  recapitalization, or IPO preparation

### The Problem They Have
Mid-market companies that have operated without a formal internal audit
function typically discover control deficiencies for the first time
during audit fieldwork or buy-side due diligence. By that point,
deficiencies have accumulated over multiple years and remediation
is time-consuming, expensive, and sometimes deal-breaking.

The most common findings at this stage:
- No formal journal entry approval workflow or documented evidence
  of review
- Account reconciliations performed inconsistently or not at all
  for non-cash accounts
- No SoD analysis — users in the ERP have accumulated permissions
  over time without regular review
- Management override of controls is undocumented and untraceable

Remediating these findings under audit or deal pressure typically
requires 3–6 months of intensive work by outside consultants at
significant cost — often $150,000 to $500,000 or more depending
on the scope of deficiencies identified.

### Why RTCM Fits
A company that deploys RTCM 12–18 months before an anticipated
audit or transaction compresses the remediation timeline dramatically.
RTCM identifies control gaps in the current environment, tracks
remediation progress in real time, and produces audit-ready
documentation of control activity — giving management and their
auditors confidence that the control environment is sound before
fieldwork begins.

### Addressable Market
- Approximately 180,000 U.S. companies with $25M–$500M in revenue
  (U.S. Census Bureau, Statistics of U.S. Businesses, 2022)
- Subset actively preparing for audit or transaction in any given
  year: estimated 15–20% based on typical M&A and audit cycle activity
- Estimated annual contract value per customer: $8,000–$20,000
  at Starter to Professional tier pricing

## Segment 3 — CPA Firms and Outsourced CFO Providers

### Profile
- Firm size: Regional or mid-size CPA firm with 10–200 professionals
- Client base: 20–100 mid-market clients, primarily private companies
- Service model: Audit, review, or outsourced accounting/CFO services
- Geographic focus: Single metro area or regional multi-office

### The Problem They Have
CPA firms serving mid-market clients are increasingly asked to provide
advisory services beyond the annual audit — including internal control
assessments, SOX readiness, and ongoing monitoring. Most firms lack
the technology infrastructure to deliver these services efficiently
at scale. Doing it manually is time-consuming and not profitable at
mid-market fee levels.

At the same time, firms face competitive pressure from national
accounting networks and technology-enabled outsourced CFO providers
who can offer broader service packages. A firm that cannot offer
continuous monitoring as part of its service package risks losing
clients to competitors who can.

### Why RTCM Fits
RTCM's white-label partner program enables CPA firms to deliver
automated internal control monitoring as a managed service — under
the firm's own branding, with RTCM powering the infrastructure.
The firm earns a 30% revenue share on all clients enrolled through
the partner portal. For a firm with 30 mid-market clients, even
50% adoption represents $150,000–$300,000 in recurring annual
revenue with minimal incremental cost.

The partner program also deepens the firm's relationship with each
client — a firm that is monitoring the client's controls year-round
is significantly harder to displace than one that shows up once a
year for fieldwork.

### Addressable Market
- Approximately 44,000 U.S. CPA firms with at least one mid-market
  client (AICPA, 2023)
- Initial target: Regional firms with 20–100 professionals and
  10+ mid-market audit or review clients
- Estimated 2,500–3,000 firms meet this profile nationally
- Each partner firm represents 10–30 potential RTCM client enrollments
